# Platform compliance: keep every video, account and channel safe

MTG, 7 November 2026: "Make sure each and every single video is compliant with community guidelines across all the
social media platforms. I don't want my videos banned or removed, or my channels shut down." Every video passes this
before it is published. Policies checked on 7 November 2026 (sources at the end); re-check the official pages at
least monthly and before any new kind of video, because platforms change their rules.

**Honest limit:** no one can promise a platform will never flag a video. Moderation is partly automated, rules change,
and even harmless videos are sometimes caught by mistake. What we can do is follow every rule that applies, use every
label the platforms ask for, keep a record, and appeal calmly when a mistake happens. Never tell MTG a video is
"100% safe"; tell him it passed the check, and what was checked.

## 1. The biggest risk in MTG's niche, and the safe structure

The platforms' fraud and scam rules target exactly the kind of opportunity content MTG's niche touches:

- **Meta (Facebook, Instagram, Threads):** prohibits investment opportunities "partly or fully based on recruitment of
  others to participate in the scheme", offers where returns are "guaranteed or risk-free", that "claim quick returns",
  that reference "successful past performance or returns", money flipping, and "unrealistic financial reward for
  unclear or minimal effort". It allows content that educates and warns about scams.
- **YouTube:** removes content "promoting 'get rich quick' investment schemes" and content "created solely to drive
  users off of YouTube to external sites, especially those hosting prohibited material". Spam, deceptive practices
  and scams cause most channel removals.
- **TikTok:** frauds and scams are prohibited; multi-level marketing ("recruiting people into pyramid-style selling
  structures") and get-rich-quick content are kept out of the For You feed, so they stop being recommended.
- **Gambling:** YouTube bans mentioning, showing or linking unapproved online gambling, and removes content promising
  guaranteed returns; TikTok prohibits promoting gambling, including paid lotteries and draws.

**So MTG's content is split in two, on purpose (this is the bridge strategy):**

| Lives on social platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, X) | Lives only on MTG's websites and in the WhatsApp tribe |
|---|---|
| Education, history, What If, Did You Know, science of money | Compensation plans that pay from recruiting (levels, matrix, global leg, matching, partner commissions) |
| Scam awareness, wallet safety, how crypto works | Sign-up links and step-by-step joining for recruitment-based programs |
| Honest checks and reviews with risks shown, no return figures | Earnings, withdrawals, returns or "past performance" of any opportunity |
| Tool trainings that do not calculate earnings from recruits | Paid draws and prizes (for example lottery-style tickets) |
| The MTG TRIBE, community, lead with value | "Join my team" pitches |
| Links to mtgtribe.com, mtgtribe.com/group and education pages | Links into recruitment or sign-up pages |

Social videos bring people into the tribe and onto the websites through education and trust; the opportunity
details are explained on MTG's own sites and in the tribe, with full risks and disclaimers. When a video's main
purpose would be to recruit into a program, it does not go on a social platform. When in doubt, it stays off.

## 2. Risk tiers (decide at the idea stage)

- **Green, publish everywhere:** education, history, science, What If, Did You Know, scam awareness, safety, how money
  and blockchains work, community and tribe content, general tool trainings.
- **Amber, publish with care:** crypto and market explainers, honest platform reviews, anything mentioning a specific
  product or company, anything with affiliate or referral links in the description. Rules: risks stated, no return
  figures, no "best", no personal advice, disclosures on, link to education pages or the tribe.
- **Red, websites and WhatsApp tribe only:** see the right column above, plus anything promising, implying or showing
  income, guaranteed or quick returns, "double your money", past returns, earnings screenshots, or paid draws.

## 3. Rules for every script, caption, title and thumbnail

- **No money promises:** never "guaranteed", "risk-free", "double your money", "passive income for life", "quick
  returns", "get rich", "financial freedom in 30 days" or results figures for an opportunity. Show risk next to every
  mention of reward. Say "education only, not financial advice".
- **No recruitment pitches** on social platforms: no "join my team", "build your downline", "earn from the people
  below you".
- **Metadata tells the truth:** the title and thumbnail must match what the video delivers (misleading metadata counts
  as spam on YouTube). Curiosity yes, false promises no.
- **Engagement is clean:** asking viewers to like, comment, share or subscribe is fine. Never reward engagement
  (no giveaways for subscribing or commenting), no "sub for sub", no identical promotional comments across other
  people's videos or groups, no "comment YES" bait on Facebook.
- **Original every time:** each video is substantially original. Never mass-produce near-identical AI videos with the
  same visuals and music (YouTube treats that as spam). The signature intro, brand bar and closing track are fine; the
  substance of every video must be new.
- **Disclose AI:** MTG's narration is his HeyGen synthetic voice, which counts as realistic altered or synthetic
  content. Always switch on the platform's AI label (section 4).
- **Disclose money interests:** when MTG can earn from a link or product, say so in the video (spoken or on screen) and
  in the description, and use the platform's paid partnership or content disclosure setting when a brand pays or the
  post promotes MTG's own business.
- **Copyright:** only MTG's own or properly licensed music, footage, images and fonts. No popular songs, film clips or
  other creators' footage. "They Call Me MTG" is MTG's own track (confirm he holds the rights before the first upload).
- **Trademarks and impersonation:** "independent guide", never implying official status; no other company's logo used
  as if it were MTG's or as an endorsement.
- **Privacy:** no personal data, wallet addresses, account numbers, faces or names of people who did not agree, in any
  frame or thumbnail.
- **Respect:** no hate, harassment, mockery of countries or groups, graphic content or political attacks. Keep history
  factual and neutral (for example, Zimbabwe's crisis is told with respect for the people who lived it).
- **Not for children:** content is for adults; on YouTube select "No, it's not made for kids"; never target under 18s.
- **No gambling:** no casinos, betting, paid lucky draws or lotteries, and no links to them.
- **No health, legal or tax claims** beyond general education.

## 5. The compliance gate (three checks)

1. **Idea:** give it a risk tier. Red ideas are planned for the websites and the tribe, not social platforms.
2. **Script:** run `scripts/compliance_scan.py <script or caption file>` (flags risky phrases and dashes), then read
   every line against section 3. Fix, do not argue with the rules.
3. **Final:** check the rendered video, thumbnail, title, description, pinned comment, links and the platform settings
   in section 4. Write the result in the posting pack: tier, labels set, disclosures, links checked, date of policy check.

If a video is flagged anyway: do not re-upload it or argue in public. Read the exact notice, fix what it names, and
appeal with a calm, factual note (education only, sources, disclosures). Track every notice in the video's folder.

## 6. South African law (not a platform rule, but it matters)

- The FSCA declared crypto assets financial products under the FAIS Act (from 19 October 2022). Giving financial advice
  without authorisation contravenes FAIS, and the FSCA watches "finfluencers" on social media.
- So MTG's videos educate; they do not advise individuals what to buy, sell or invest in. "Not financial advice" helps
  but does not turn advice into education: avoid personal recommendations ("you should put R1,000 into...").
- Disclose commercial relationships (advertising code) and protect members' personal data (POPIA).
- Recommend to MTG: a once-off review of his income-stream content by a South African compliance professional. This
  skill is not legal advice.
