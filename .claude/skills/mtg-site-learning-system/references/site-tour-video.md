# The site tour video (standard on every website, MTG 6 November 2026)

The very first thing a visitor sees at the top of the dashboard is a video that shows them around the whole website,
click by click: from the landing page to the dashboard, then every page, so they always know where to find what. It is
as polished as every other MTG video. A visitor who knows their way around stays, learns and acts; a lost visitor leaves.

## When

Made last for each website, once every page (and ideally every explainer) is finished, because it shows the real site.
Re-record it whenever the menu, the page list or the main buttons change, and note in the site's `PLAN.md` the date the
tour was recorded, so it never shows a site that no longer exists.

## Where

Every MTG opportunity has the same shape: a landing page, a **Dashboard** button on it (sometimes "Learn more"),
and the dashboard with every page behind it. The tour belongs to the dashboard.

- At the very top of the dashboard, above everything else, replacing MTG's photo card where the dashboard has one
  today (MTG, 6 November 2026), in the laptop frame (16:9), closing
  itself at the end like every MTG player, with a poster that says what it is ("Your 3-minute tour of the site").
- A short "New here? Take the tour" link on the landing page and in the menu, pointing to it.
- Listed first on the site's videos page, and as a "Take the site tour" button on the PDF companion's cover.

## Route (one chapter each, in this order)

1. **The landing page:** what it is for, and the exact button that takes you to the dashboard.
2. **The dashboard:** each card or section, top to bottom, one sentence each, and where "Start here" is.
3. **The menu:** how to open it on a laptop and on a phone (menu button, side drawer, edge pill), and how it is grouped.
4. **Every page, in menu order:** what it is for in one sentence, its key button or tool, and where its video is.
   Group small pages (FAQ, disclaimer, downloads) into one quick chapter.
5. **Getting back:** how to return to the dashboard or the home page from anywhere.
6. **Your first step:** the one action to take now, with MTG's link on the end card.

Keep it as short as covering every page allows: one beat per page, usually 2 to 4 minutes. Show a chapter bar on screen
(as in the ALTRUVA welcome tour) and list the chapters under the player, so viewers can jump straight to a page.

## Click by click

- Record the real, live site with a browser (Playwright), in a clean state: no login, no saved progress or ticks, no
  personal data, a neutral example in every form. Record the laptop view for the tour, and the phone view wherever
  navigation differs (the menu button, the drawer, the edge pill, back to top), because most visitors use phones: show
  the phone in MTG's slim phone frame beside the laptop, or as a short phone segment.
- Before every click: the pointer or a tap ring moves to the target, the target gets a soft highlight, and the caption
  and the voice say its exact on-screen label ("Tap **Start here**"). Zoom in gently on small targets.
- After every click: show where you landed and light up that page's title, as the success check, before moving on.
- One focal point at a time, the same icons, emojis, sound effects and brand bar as the other videos, captions with the
  active word lit, and only the last line of his theme ("Let's get this crypto, wherever I go!") at the very end.

## Voice

A tour mostly needs new lines (page names and where to tap change from site to site), so it usually costs credits:
about 400 to 600 words for 3 to 4 minutes, roughly 7 to 14 credits at the safe budget in `voice-and-credits.md`. Reuse
recorded lines for page descriptions where the voice bank has them, show MTG the script with "recorded" and "new"
marked and the credit total, and let him decide (a captions-and-music version with only recorded lines is the free option).

## Extra use

Cut the tour into one short clip per page ("how to use this page") that can sit at the top of each page.

## Check before showing MTG

Every click in the video matches the live site on the day it was recorded, every label is spelled exactly as on screen,
no personal data appears in any frame, and the dashboard still passes the sideways-scroll check with the player in place.
