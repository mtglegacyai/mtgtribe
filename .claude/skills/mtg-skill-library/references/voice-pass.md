# Voice pass: words that sound like MTG, not like a machine

Use on every script line, page, caption, post description, prospect message, PDF and email before MTG sees it.
His voice: warm and patient, like family sitting beside you, plain words, one step at a time, honest, no hype.

Sources: Stop Slop by Hardik Pandya (MIT, `hardikpandya/stop-slop` at 8da1f030185b) and the writing rules in Marketing
Skills' copywriting playbook by Corey Haines (MIT, `coreyhaines31/marketingskills` at 8fd2fcade2fd). Adapted for MTG.

## Cut these on sight

- **Empty openers** that announce the point instead of making it: "Here's the thing", "Here's why", "Here's what",
  "The truth is", "The reality is", "It turns out", "Let me be clear", "I'm going to be honest".
  Say the point. "Here's why this matters: your coins stay in your wallet" becomes "Your coins stay in your wallet."
- **Emphasis crutches:** "Let that sink in", "Make no mistake", "Full stop", "Period", "This matters because".
- **Buzzwords:** game-changer, leverage, seamless, navigate, landscape, deep dive, revolutionary, cutting-edge,
  "in today's fast-paced world". Use the plain word: use, handle, easy, field, look closely.
  Exception: a product's own word stays (a ClickBaitPays level that "unlocks" is the platform's term).
- **The fake contrast formula:** "It's not X, it's Y", "Not because X. Because Y.", "X isn't the problem. Y is.",
  "stops being X and starts being Y". Say Y plainly.
- **Vague claims:** "The benefits are huge", "The implications are significant". Name the actual thing: "$0.48 per
  click", "your account is opened in your own name".
- **Things doing what people do:** "the market rewards you", "the opportunity finds you". Name who or what really
  does it: "buyers pay", "the smart contract pays out every 24 hours", "you share your link".
- **Empty adverbs:** really, just, literally, actually, simply, truly, genuinely, honestly, when they add nothing.
  Keep an adverb that carries meaning ("pays out automatically").
- **A quotable one-liner closing every paragraph.** One strong line lands; five in a row sound written by a machine.
  Vary how paragraphs end.
- **Stacked fragments in written copy:** "Simple. Fast. Powerful." Write a sentence. (Punch words on screen in a
  video are a different thing and stay: his "WE LEARN BEST WHEN WE TEACH OTHERS" endings are kinetic text.)
- **Em and en dashes:** never (already MTG's rule). Use a comma, colon, full stop or parentheses.

## Keep doing (where MTG's style beats the source)

The sources say "skip hand-holding", "trust the reader", "two items beat three", "never start a sentence with How or
What", "kill all adverbs", "remove exclamation marks". For MTG's audience those rules would hurt:

- **Hold their hand.** His visitors are often new to crypto and to online income. One step at a time, exact on-screen
  labels, explain one word at a time with an everyday picture (bus, queue, seats), and say where the picture stops.
- **"Three things you'll learn" stays.** It is part of his promo formula (mtg-promo-video).
- **"How to ..." headings and question FAQs stay.** They are how people search, and how AI answers pick passages
  (see `ai-search.md`).
- **"Let's Get This Crypto! 💪" keeps its exclamation mark,** and so do warm greetings. Elsewhere, earn each one.
- **Warm reassurance stays where it helps a beginner act** ("Take your time, you can pause the video"). Cut it only
  when it is filler.

## Plain words

Simple over complex ("use", not "utilise"; "help", not "facilitate"). Specific over vague (a number with its $, a
time, a name). Active over passive ("you tap Join", not "Join is tapped"). Claims only the facts support: never a made-up
number, balance, result or testimonial (MTG's rule, and the copywriting playbook's).

## The five-point check (before delivery)

Score 1 to 10 each. Below 38 out of 50: rewrite the weak parts, then score again.

| | Ask |
|---|---|
| Clear | Would a first-time visitor on a phone get it on the first read? |
| Direct | Does each sentence say something, or announce that something is coming? |
| Warm | Does it sound like MTG beside them, patient and kind, with no pressure? |
| Human | Read aloud: any phrase from the "Cut these" list? Any line only a machine would write? |
| Tight | Anything that could go without losing meaning or warmth? |

The source's pass mark was 35 of 50; MTG's bar is higher (his 18 Nov 2026 rule: the best possible result, every time).

## Not taken, and why

- "Every sentence needs a human subject; no passive voice ever": too absolute for step instructions and on-chain facts.
- "No lazy extremes (every, always, never)": MTG's standing rules use them on purpose ("every page", "never a fake
  balance"). Cut them only where they are vague.
- The source's own example uses an em dash. Ours never do.
