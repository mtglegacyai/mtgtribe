# Source: "OlyLife Tera P90 Review: Two Doctors Reveal the Truth and Benefits" (YouTube, sent by MTG 14 Nov 2026)

About 39 minutes. Reposted by "Andrew" from the **Essential Therapies podcast** of **Dr. Cindy Hodgson** (physical
therapist and exercise physiologist) with **Dr. Gary Smith** (chiropractor in Toledo, Ohio, 40 years in practice,
also a hypnotherapist and "energy medicine" practitioner). Extracted from the transcript MTG pasted; MTG's copy is
on his Google Drive.

**Read this first:** both doctors say on camera that they offer the machine in their practices **and sell it**
("we both have sources for you to be able to get one"). It is a sales conversation by honest-sounding people, not a
study. Use it for practical tips and real-life feelings, never for health claims. Their footage belongs to them:
do not put clips on MTG's site without written permission (a link to their video is fine).

## What MTG's pages CAN use (calm, practical, matches `olylife-facts.md`)

**1. "Everyone feels it differently, and some feel nothing at first."** Strongest point for MTG, because it matches
his own first session.
- Dr. Hodgson: "Everyone has a different sensation... I could feel this frequency happening... But not everyone feels
  that and that's okay because we're all unique."
- Dr. Smith, who was not impressed at first ("a plastic tub with a couple metal plates"), felt his feet "like I'm
  walking on a cloud" after leaving his first 30-minute session. Compare MTG, a few hours after his first: feet
  "light... like fresh air going through them".
- Use on the page: an FAQ "I didn't feel anything. Is that normal?" with a calm answer: many people feel warmth or
  tingling, some feel nothing at first; that is common; keep a short note of how you feel.

**2. Start low and slow.** Their own rule, and it agrees with the official instructions.
- "You got to start low and slow. It may be level one or two for seven, eight minutes, and gradually work up."
- Their stories: a friend put straight on level 8 was sore the next day; another felt "too much" at level 5 after
  starting fine at 2; a man's big toe twitched at level 2 and he stopped after 6 minutes; Dr. Hodgson's mother began
  at 1 to 2 minutes, twice a week, and built up to 10 minutes.
- Use on the page (calm info note, no warning box): "First session? Level 1 or 2. Your body sets the pace. Go up
  one step at a time over the following sessions."

**3. How often (their routine, within the official limits).**
- The machine is designed for up to 60 minutes a day: 30 minutes twice a day (they also split sessions into shorter
  ones). Dr. Smith does 15 minutes twice a day: a stronger level (6 or 7) in the morning, level 2 in the evening.
- Some of their patients use it once a week or every two weeks and still notice something; regular use is what they
  recommend.
- Fits the facts file: 30 minutes maximum per session, up to twice a day, hours apart. MTG's centre asks for at least
  6 hours between sessions.

**4. The levels (their description, not an official spec).** The Tera-P90 has 10 levels. They describe 1 to 3 as
gentle and calming (mostly PEMF) and 8 to 10 as stronger (more terahertz). Say it as "lower levels feel gentle,
higher levels feel stronger", not as a technical fact.

**5. What people report feeling during a session.** Warmth or heat building up (turn it down if it gets too much),
tingling, a toe twitching, a calm or meditative feeling, "I slept better". Fine on the page as **what people report**,
next to MTG's own log.

**6. Easy to fit into a day.** "You can just sit there... I've had my dinner while I have my feet on that." One
machine shared by the whole family. (Contraindications in the facts file still apply to each person.)

**7. Lines that fit MTG's message without claiming anything.**
- "You have to listen to your own body." (Dr. Smith)
- "Testimonials... everybody is so different." (both, about not over-weighting stories)
- Dr. Smith: "I hesitate to tell patients what the potential benefit is... because I don't want to give them false
  hope." Good honest framing for the page.
