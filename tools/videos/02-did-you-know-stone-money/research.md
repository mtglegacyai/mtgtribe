## Thumbnail and cover patterns

The stone alone (the hole in the middle is instantly recognisable), its size next to a person, few words ("MONEY THAT
WAS TOO BIG TO MOVE").

## The gap

- No strong Short tells the sunken-stone moment, the most surprising part, in under a minute.
- Most never connect it clearly to how a blockchain works today, or say where the comparison stops.
- No conversation built in.

## Our "better than" plan

1. The sunken stone as the very first image and line (the payoff of every long video, delivered in 3 seconds).
2. One idea, one visual at a time in 3D: the stone, the moving owner tag, the sinking, the chain of blocks.
3. A clear, honest bridge to blockchain, with the difference stated (computers, not memory).
4. A real question that splits opinion: "Would you accept money you can never see?"
5. A loop ending (back to the sinking stone) and a series hook to part two (money cheaper than paper, from video 01).
6. MTG's circle photo and voice: a face and a person behind the facts.

## Facts and sources

- Rai stones as money on Yap, ownership changing without moving the stone, the sunken-stone story told as local
  legend (Furness, The Island of Stone Money, 1910; Friedman, 1991): https://www.moneyness.ca/2013/01/yap-stones-and-myth-of-fiat-money.html
- Size about 3.6 metres (12 feet) for the largest: Financial Times episode title above; Penn Museum object record:
  https://penn.museum/collections/object/303730
- Yap uses the US dollar today; stones still used in traditional exchanges (sources differ on how often).
