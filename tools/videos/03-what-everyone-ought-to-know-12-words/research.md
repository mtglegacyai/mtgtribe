## How ours will be better

1. The danger in the first line ("whoever has them, has your money"), not a definition.
2. One everyday picture: the master key to your house, and exactly where that picture stops (a copied key works from
   anywhere, silently, and there is no bank to call).
3. The scam named plainly: fake "support" asking for the words, and the one rule (real support never asks).
4. What to do, in one line people can remember (paper, offline, never into a link someone sent you).
5. A safe prompt that starts real stories ("Has anyone ever asked you for your twelve words?") with an on-screen
   warning never to post the words in the comments.
6. Words are always shown masked (••••) so no frame shows a usable phrase.

## Facts (verified 8 November 2026)

- Self-custody wallets give a 12 or 24 word recovery phrase (BIP-39) that restores the wallet on another device:
  https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0039.mediawiki
- Anyone with the phrase can restore the wallet and move the funds; blockchain transfers cannot be reversed by a bank.
  Wallet makers say they will never ask for it and that anyone who does is a scammer: MetaMask
  https://support.metamask.io/stay-safe/safety-in-web3/basic-safety-and-security-tips-for-metamask and Trust Wallet
  https://support.trustwallet.com/en/articles/752048-how-to-spot-and-avoid-crypto-wallet-scams (both read 8 Nov 2026;
  Trust Wallet adds that its staff never message you first, and that paid "recovery" offers are scams).
- Search results for these brands are full of fake "support" pages and phone numbers: proof the scam is real and live.
