## The gap

A story millions have clicked on, but almost every version tells it as "the most expensive mistake ever". The real
lesson is missing: this was the day Bitcoin first bought something real, and Laszlo himself says it made Bitcoin real.
No warm, under-a-minute version with his actual words on screen and no regret or pressure.

## How ours will be better

1. The whole story in the first five seconds: 10,000 Bitcoin for two pizzas.
2. His real forum post on screen, word for word, with its date.
3. The numbers then and now, dated and sourced, with "the past promises nothing".
4. The lesson in his own words ("made [Bitcoin] real"), not a regret story.
5. A fun question everyone can answer: the pizzas or the coins?
6. A share prompt that fits ("a friend who loves pizza"), then the tribe.

## Facts (verified 13 November 2026)

- The offer: Bitcointalk forum, user laszlo, 18 May 2010, 12:35 AM: "I'll pay 10,000 bitcoins for a couple of
  pizzas.. like maybe 2 large ones so I have some left over for the next day." The report, 22 May 2010, 07:17 PM: "I
  just want to report that I successfully traded 10,000 bitcoins for pizza." and "Thanks jercos!" Source read:
  https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=137.0
- Who: Laszlo Hanyecz bought the pizzas through Jeremy Sturdivant, a 19-year-old with the username "jercos"; Papa
  John's made them; the 10,000 BTC could be sold for about $41 at the time; 22 May is celebrated as Bitcoin Pizza Day.
  In a 2019 CBS interview with Anderson Cooper he said the transaction "made [bitcoin] real for some people. It
  certainly did for me." Source read: CoinDesk, "What Is Bitcoin Pizza Day?",
  https://www.coindesk.com/learn/what-is-bitcoin-pizza-day/
- Today's price: about $71,000 per Bitcoin on 13 November 2026 (OKX $72,171, Bitstack $71,405, Coinglass $71,469,
  Kriptofoni $70,901 at different times that day), so 10,000 BTC is about $710 million. Said as "about seven hundred
  million"; on screen with the date.
- "Barely a year old": Bitcoin's first block was mined in January 2009; the pizza was bought in May 2010, about 16
  months later (on screen: "Bitcoin is 1 year old").

## Limits

The price is a snapshot of one volatile day and is shown with its date and "the past promises nothing about the
future". "Hardly anyone had spent it" is used instead of "nobody": this is the first widely known purchase of goods
with Bitcoin, and small trades before it cannot be ruled out. No advice to buy anything.
