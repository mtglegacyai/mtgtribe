## The gap

Gold pulls millions of views, but the winners are long (4 minutes to over an hour). The "true scale" idea is proven
and recent, yet nobody plays it as a one-minute guessing game that lets the viewer lock in an answer, then ties the
surprise to a lesson they can use (scarcity, and why Bitcoin copied it).

## How ours will be better

1. A game, not a lecture: three options, a countdown, then the answer. People comment their guess.
2. A big true number in the first two seconds (6,000 years), and the question in the first four.
3. The answer shown to scale twice: beside a 7-floor building and on a soccer field.
4. A source on screen (World Gold Council, 2026), not "they say".
5. A lesson that links gold to Bitcoin (21 million) and one tip to keep: ask how rare it really is.
6. A share prompt with a reason ("a friend who thinks there's a mountain of gold out there"), then the tribe.

## Facts (verified 14 November 2026)

- All the gold ever mined: the World Gold Council's page "How much gold has been mined?" says "around 222,600 tonnes
  of gold has been mined throughout history" and that one cube of it "would only measure around 22.6 metres on each
  side"; its above-ground stock table lists 224,200 tonnes at the end of the third quarter of 2026 (the end-2025
  figure was 219,891 tonnes). Said as "more than two hundred and twenty thousand tonnes" and "about twenty-three
  metres wide"; on screen "220,000+ tonnes" and "≈ 22.6 m on each side". Source read:
  https://www.gold.org/goldhub/data/how-much-gold
- The arithmetic, checked: gold weighs 19.32 tonnes per cubic metre, so 224,200 tonnes is about 11,600 cubic metres,
  a cube of 22.6 m (222,600 tonnes gives 22.6 m too).
- A 7-floor building: at about 3.2 m per floor, seven floors are about 22.6 m. Said as "as tall as a seven-floor
  building" (an everyday comparison, not a measurement of one building).
- A soccer field: the common full-size field is 105 x 68 m (7,140 square metres). The cube's footprint is 22.6 x
  22.6 m, about 511 square metres, about 7% of the field. Across every size allowed for international matches
  (100 to 110 m by 64 to 75 m) it is 6% to 8%: always "less than a tenth".
- "Humans have chased gold for six thousand years": the gold of the Varna necropolis in Bulgaria is dated to about
  4600 to 4200 BC, more than 6,000 years ago. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_Necropolis
- "Nobody can print more": new gold only comes from mining (and recycling of gold that already exists).
- Bitcoin: the supply limit of 21 million coins (strictly just under 21 million) is a rule in Bitcoin's own code.
  Sources: https://help.blockstream.com/education/economics/bitcoin-economics/why-is-the-bitcoin-supply-limited and
  https://www.unchained.com/blog/bitcoin-source-code-21-million

## Limits

The total is the World Gold Council's best estimate, not an exact count, and it grows a little every year as mining
continues, so the video says "more than" and "about". The video gives no price, no forecast and no advice to buy
gold or Bitcoin; the tip is a question to ask, and the screen says "Education, not financial advice. Prices move."
