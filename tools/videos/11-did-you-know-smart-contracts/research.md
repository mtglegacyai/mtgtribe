## The gap

The vending machine picture is proven: Bloomberg's 1:23 explainer has 4.1 million views. But it is four years old,
it does not say where the picture stops, and it gives the viewer nothing to do. Nobody pairs the explanation with the
one habit that keeps a beginner safe (never approve what you don't understand).

## How ours will be better

1. Three crossed-out middlemen and a big, dated number in the first five seconds.
2. The vending machine drawn and animated: pay, pick, drop, synced to the words.
3. "If this, then that" typed as code, with a plain example.
4. An honest "where the picture stops" (a broken machine gets fixed; a live contract usually can't be).
5. One tip shown on a real-looking wallet approval screen: if you can't explain it, don't sign it.
6. A share prompt with a reason ("a friend who thinks crypto is just coins"), a save prompt, then the tribe.

## Facts (verified 16 November 2026)

- "About seventy billion dollars is locked in them right now": DefiLlama's own data (api.llama.fi, historical chain
  TVL) shows the total value locked in DeFi at $71.6 billion on 16 November 2026 ($79.1 billion on 9 November). DeFi
  apps are smart contracts, so this is money locked in smart contracts; it leaves out other contracts (for example
  most stablecoin balances), so "about seventy billion" is a fair floor. On screen with source and date.
- Definition, the vending machine picture and the history: ethereum.org says smart contracts are "computer programs
  stored on the blockchain" that follow "if this then that" logic, that "Nick Szabo coined the term" (an introduction
  in 1994, more in 1996), uses the vending machine as the picture ("specific inputs guarantee predetermined outputs"),
  says the code "cannot be changed once created", and that they "remove the need for trusted intermediaries". Source
  read: https://ethereum.org/smart-contracts/
- "Usually can't be" fixed: some contracts are built to be upgradeable by their owners, so the script says
  "usually".
- The tip: ethereum.org warns about "blind signing", approving a transaction without understanding what it will do.
  The wallet screen in the video is a drawing of a typical "allow this site to spend all your tokens" request, with a
  made-up site name.

## Limits

The DeFi figure moves every day (it fell from $79.1 billion to $71.6 billion in a week), so the screen shows the date
and the voice says "about". No app, token or platform is named or recommended. The video gives no advice to buy
anything.
